# -Can-my-spouse-get-a-work-permit-if-I-work-in-Canada-

If you’re working in Canada and your spouse is planning to join you, one of the first questions you may have is: **Can my spouse get a work permit too?**

The answer is **sometimes, yes**.

Canada allows eligible spouses and common-law partners of certain foreign workers to apply for an **open work permit**. However, the rules are not the same for everyone. Your occupation, work permit, remaining work permit validity, and [immigration pathway](https://canada.asraimmigration.ca/) can all affect whether your spouse qualifies.

And since the rules changed in 2025, it’s important to look at the current requirements rather than relying on older information.

## What Is a Spousal Open Work Permit?

An open work permit allows your spouse or common-law partner to work for most employers in Canada without needing a job offer from a specific employer.

Unlike an employer-specific work permit, an open work permit is generally not tied to one particular job or employer.

For families, this can make a big difference. Instead of one person working while the other waits for their own employment authorization, an eligible spouse may be able to work and build their own Canadian experience.

But there is an important point to remember:

**Being married to someone who works in Canada does not automatically make you eligible for an open work permit.**

You need to meet the requirements of the applicable immigration measure.

## When Can My Spouse Get an Open Work Permit?

As of January 21, 2025, Canada's rules became more restrictive for spouses of temporary foreign workers.

In general, spouses of foreign workers may qualify if the principal worker meets specific requirements related to their occupation and work authorization.

For example, under the current rules, spouses of workers in **TEER 0 or 1 occupations** may qualify if the other requirements are met.

Some workers in **TEER 2 or 3 occupations** can also make their spouse eligible, but only certain occupations are included.

This means that simply saying, “I work in Canada,” isn't enough to determine eligibility.

Your **NOC code and TEER category** matter.

## How Long Must My Work Permit Be Valid?

For many spouses of high-skilled foreign workers, your work permit or authorization to work generally needs to remain valid for **at least 16 months** when your spouse submits their application.

For example, imagine you are working in Canada on a valid work permit and have 18 months remaining on it.

If your occupation and other circumstances meet the requirements, your spouse may be able to apply for an open work permit.

On the other hand, if your work permit is going to expire soon, your spouse may not qualify under this particular measure.

There are exceptions and separate rules for certain immigration programs, so it's important to look at your specific situation.

## What If I Work in a TEER 2 or TEER 3 Job?

This is where things can get a little more complicated.

Not every TEER 2 or TEER 3 occupation automatically makes a spouse eligible for an open work permit.

IRCC has identified specific occupations and sectors where spouses may qualify. These include certain jobs connected to areas such as:

* Healthcare
* Construction
* Natural and applied sciences
* Natural resources
* Education
* Sports
* Certain military-related occupations

The exact eligible occupations can change, so you should check the current IRCC list using your **NOC code**, rather than relying only on your job title.

## What If I Have a Lower-Skilled Job?

This is one of the biggest changes families need to understand.

Under the current rules, spouses of foreign workers in lower-skilled occupations generally **cannot apply for a new open work permit under the general family-member measure**, unless another specific immigration pathway or exception applies.

There are, however, specific situations where family members of workers in lower-skilled occupations may still qualify—for example, where the worker is on an eligible pathway to permanent residence.

So if your job falls under TEER 4 or 5, don't assume that your spouse has no options. Instead, check whether your work permit and immigration pathway fall under one of the exceptions.

## What If I Am Applying for Permanent Residence?

This can change the situation.

Certain workers who are already on an eligible [**pathway to permanent residence**](https://canada.asraimmigration.ca/) may have different rules for their family members.

For example, IRCC lists several eligible PR pathways under its family-member work permit measures, including certain Provincial Nominee Program streams and other economic immigration programs.

There are specific requirements for both the principal applicant and the spouse, so having a PR application or being on a PR pathway does not automatically guarantee a work permit for your spouse.

## What If I Am an International Student Instead of a Worker?

If you're actually in Canada as an international student, the rules are different.

Since January 21, 2025, spouses and common-law partners of international students may generally qualify for an open work permit only in certain situations.

For example, the student may need to be enrolled in:

* A master's program lasting 16 months or longer
* A doctoral program
* Certain eligible professional degree programs

There are additional requirements, so spouses of students should check the current IRCC criteria before applying.

## Does My Spouse Need a Job Offer?

Generally, an **open work permit does not require a job offer**.

That's one of the main differences between an open work permit and an employer-specific work permit.

If your spouse qualifies for an open work permit, they can generally look for employment after receiving the permit without first obtaining a job offer from a Canadian employer.

However, eligibility for the open work permit comes first. A job offer isn't a substitute for meeting the immigration requirements.

## Can My Spouse Apply From Outside Canada?

Depending on your situation, your spouse may be able to apply for a work permit before coming to Canada or may need to apply separately after arriving.

IRCC provides different application options depending on where the principal worker and family member are located and which work permit program applies.

If you and your spouse are applying together, make sure you understand which applications should be submitted and what supporting documents are required.

## What Documents Might My Spouse Need?

The exact document checklist depends on the application, but your spouse may need documents such as:

* A valid passport
* Marriage certificate or proof of common-law relationship
* A copy of your work permit
* Proof of your employment
* Your employment letter
* Documents showing your occupation and NOC code
* Proof of your status in Canada
* Forms required by IRCC
* Other documents requested during the application process

If your spouse is already in Canada, their temporary status can also matter. IRCC says family members applying from inside Canada must meet specific status requirements or qualify under the relevant restoration rules.

## What If My Spouse Already Has a Work Permit?

If your spouse already has an open work permit and is in Canada, different rules may apply when extending it.

For example, IRCC allows certain family members in Canada to extend their work permits where their current permit expires earlier than the principal applicant's permit, provided they meet the applicable conditions and apply before their existing permit expires.

So, don't wait until the last few days before your spouse's work permit expires. Check the extension requirements early.

## A Simple Example

Let's say you're working in Canada as a software engineer.

You have:

* A valid Canadian work permit
* A TEER 1 occupation
* More than 16 months remaining on your work permit
* Plans to continue living and working in Canada

Your spouse may be eligible to apply for an open work permit, assuming they meet the other requirements.

Now consider a different situation where you're working in a TEER 4 occupation and aren't on an eligible pathway to permanent residence.

In that case, your spouse may **not** qualify under the general family-member open work permit rules.

That's why your occupation and immigration status matter so much.

## What Should You Check Before Applying?

Before your spouse submits an application, check these points:

**1. Your work permit**

Is it valid, and how much time remains?

**2. Your NOC code**

Don't rely only on your job title. Confirm the NOC that applies to your actual job duties.

**3. Your TEER category**

This can determine whether your occupation falls within an eligible category.

**4. Your immigration pathway**

Are you simply working temporarily, or are you on an eligible pathway toward permanent residence?

**5. Your spouse's status**

If your spouse is already in Canada, check whether they have valid temporary status or qualify under the applicable restoration or maintained-status rules.

**6. Current IRCC requirements**

Immigration policies can change, so always check the latest requirements before submitting an application.
